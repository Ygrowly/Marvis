Coding agents are extraordinarily good now and getting better fast. The interesting consequence is that **the hard part of engineering moved from writing code to deciding whether to trust it, which makes review the most leveraged skill in software right now**. How you approach it depends enormously on who you are: a solo developer with no users and a team maintaining a ten-year-old application are not solving the same problem.

I am more optimistic about agentic engineering than I have ever been. The agents are genuinely good, they get better every month, and on an ordinary week I now ship things I would not have attempted in the same time a year ago. This write-up is a map of where the interesting work went, because it did move, and most teams have not fully caught up to where.

Code review used to work because of a happy accident of relative speed. A senior engineer could read code faster than a junior could write it, so review kept pace without anyone designing it to, and the team absorbed how the system fit together as a side effect of reading each other’s diffs. A lot of that was not deliberate. It fell out of a single fact: writing code was the slow, expensive part, and reading it was cheap and fast.

That fact no longer holds. An agent will produce a thousand lines of often solid, well-formatted code in less time than it takes me to read this paragraph, while a human’s reading speed has not changed since roughly the day we started staring at screens for a living. So the constraint moved downstream, to the one step that did not get faster: a person being confident the change is right. I do not think that is a loss. It is the most leveraged place in software to be good right now, and it is where I have put most of my attention this year.

There is a happy twist here that shapes the rest of this piece. **The same tools generating all that extra code are also the best thing I have for keeping up with it.** On my own projects, including the popular open-source ones, I now point Claude Code or Codex at a batch of incoming PRs and have them triage the queue for me, and that has genuinely changed how I spend my time. So this is not an anti-AI argument, and I will come back to exactly how I use it.

It is also not a data dump, and not another round of whether letting a model write your code is wonderful or the end of the craft, because that framing is useless. The only answer that survives contact with a real codebase is that it depends entirely on who you are. A developer vibe-coding a side project a dozen people will ever run, and a team keeping a ten-year-old enterprise system alive for another quarter, share almost no constraints worth naming, and most of the advice in circulation is really one of those two people telling the other how to live.

## What the 2026 data actually shows

**The productivity gains from AI are real, but raw output overstates them: about four times the code for a tenth more delivered value. The gap between those numbers is review work, which is exactly why review is where the leverage now sits.**

For a couple of years this was anecdote and argument. It is now measured at scale, by organizations with no shared agenda and in several cases competing commercial interests, and the measurements keep pointing the same way: AI pushes output sharply up, and pushes both quality and reviewability down.

[Faros AI](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways) instrumented 22,000 developers across 4,000 teams and tracked what happened as teams moved from low to high AI adoption. This is March 2026 data, about as current as anything here. The upside is real and worth stating plainly: developers merge considerably more PRs and complete more work, and throughput per engineer climbs. Then the rest of the report:

- code churn up **861%**
- the incidents-to-PR ratio up **242.7%**
- the per-developer defect rate up from **9% to 54%**
- median review duration up **441.5%**, with time-to-first-review and average review time both roughly doubling
- PRs merged with **zero review up 31.3%**

The last figure is the one I find hardest to dismiss, because nobody chose it. There was no decision to stop reviewing. **Reviewers simply could not keep pace with the volume, so code began merging unread, and that became normal.** The detail I keep returning to is that teams with mature, disciplined engineering practices were hit just as hard as everyone else. Good process did not protect them, because the volume arrived faster than any process was designed to absorb.

One caveat to hold throughout: CodeRabbit and Faros both sell into this market, so their framing is not disinterested. That does not make the numbers wrong, the effect sizes are large and consistent across unrelated sources, but vendor research deserves to be read with that in mind.

[CodeRabbit](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251217666881/en/CodeRabbits-State-of-AI-vs-Human-Code-Generation-Report-Finds-That-AI-Written-Code-Produces-1.7x-More-Issues-Than-Human-Code) studied 470 open source PRs in December 2025, 320 AI-coauthored and 150 human-only, and found the AI changes carried roughly **1.7x more issues**: logic and correctness problems up about 75%, security issues 1.5 to 2x more common, readability problems more than tripling. Their AI director David Loker described these as “predictable, measurable weaknesses that organizations must actively mitigate”. Predictable is the operative word. These are known, locatable weaknesses, which is good news: it means a review process, human or automated, can be aimed straight at them.

[GitClear](https://www.gitclear.com/research/ai_tool_impact_on_developer_productive_output_from_2022_to_2025) has interesting data here too. In their productivity data through 2025, daily AI users produce around **4x the raw output** of non-users, but measured against their own output a year earlier, the real productivity gain is only about **12%**. You are generating roughly four times the code for something like a tenth more delivered value, and a human still has to review all four times of it. To GitClear’s credit, Bill Harding is explicit that some of even that 12% is selection bias, because stronger developers concentrated in the AI cohort. The gap between 4x the code and a tenth more value is the review problem stated in one line.

[GitHub](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/agent-pull-requests-are-everywhere-heres-how-to-review-them/) reports that Copilot review has now run over 60 million reviews, a 10x increase in under a year, and more than one in five reviews on the platform involves an agent. This is no longer a niche practice. It is how code gets made.

Four datasets, four methods, one conclusion. We poured machine-speed output into a system built for human-speed work. The bottleneck did not disappear; it [moved to verification](https://addyosmani.com/blog/verification-bottleneck/), and review is where that bill comes due.

## Everyone is solving a different problem

**How much review a change needs depends almost entirely on its blast radius, and most advice you read was written by someone operating at a very different one.**

Almost all the alarming data above comes from enterprise telemetry and from open source maintainers being overwhelmed. It is entirely real if that is your situation. If you are one person shipping something a handful of people will ever run, much of it simply does not apply to you, and you should not be made to feel otherwise.

Three variables determine where you sit:

- **blast radius**: what happens when it breaks. Nothing, or angry users and money and PII on the line.
- **how long the code lives**: a throwaway prototype you might rewrite next week, or a codebase you will maintain for years.
- **how many people need to understand it**: just you holding the whole thing in your head, or a team that has to share ownership over time.

Run the same diff through those three and “good review” means genuinely different things.

If you are working solo on a greenfield project with no users, review’s second job, distributing knowledge across a team, does not exist for you. You are the team.

The reasonable move is to lean hard on [tests and automation](https://addyosmani.com/blog/verification-bottleneck/), review the parts that genuinely matter, and accept a lighter touch on the rest. Duplication and churn cost far less when the code may not exist in a month and nobody is paged at 3am when it breaks. The catch, and people learn this one painfully, is that it only works if the tests are real. Skipping review without a safety net does not remove the work, it [defers it](https://addyosmani.com/blog/intent-debt/) at a higher price, and standards slip when no one is there to push back. No users is permission to defer review. It is not permission to skip verification.

Then the project gets users. This is the dangerous middle, and the crossing is rarely noticed at the time. Review's bug-catching role suddenly matters, because bugs now hurt people, and its knowledge-sharing role switches on, because it is no longer only you. Teams keep their solo-era habits a few months too long, and then there is a postmortem and the Faros numbers stop being a chart and become their own dashboard.

At the far end is the large organization with an old codebase and many users. Here every alarming figure lands at full strength. A change nobody understood is [comprehension debt](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/) that becomes someone’s on-call incident. Review is doing several jobs at once, and the volume of agent output quietly breaks all of them. The Faros finding about mature teams is aimed squarely here.

So the point is not "enterprises should be cautious and solo developers can relax". It is that the purpose of review changes with your position, so the rules have to change with it. Bolt an enterprise’s locked-down, multi-agent, evidence-required pipeline onto a two-person prototype and you have added friction for no benefit. Run “tests pass, ship it” on a payments system and you have built an incident generator with a green checkmark on top. Most bad advice in this space is one position on that spectrum prescribing to another.

## What review is actually for now

**Review was built to check an author's reasoning and catch bugs + knowledge share with the team. An agent does reason, but that reasoning is usually thrown away rather than attached to the code, so the reviewer has to reconstruct a rationale that never made it into the diff. The good news: that is a tooling problem, and capturing the reasoning makes review dramatically easier.进行代码审查的目的是为了检验开发人员的推理过程、发现其中的错误，并与团队成员分享知识。虽然开发人员会进行各种推理，但这些推理通常不会被记录下来或嵌入到代码中。因此，审查人员不得不重新梳理那些没有被记录下来的推理过程。不过，这其实是个工具层面的问题；只要能把这些推理过程记录下来，代码审查的工作就会变得容易得多。**

This is the part that genuinely changed, and I think it is underappreciated.这才是真正发生改变的部分，不过我觉得这一点没有得到应有的重视。

When a human writes code, intent comes along for free. The reasoning, the alternatives weighed and discarded, lived in the author’s head, and review was you checking that reasoning. Modern agents do reason, often visibly, producing thinking traces and weighing options and explaining themselves as they go. The catch is that this reasoning is usually discarded the moment the diff is produced. It is rarely captured, rarely attached to the PR, and in any case it is the agent’s reasoning about how to implement the task, not a human’s judgment about whether it was the right task to begin with. So review shifts from checking reasoning that sits in front of you to reconstructing intent that never got written down, which is harder and slower, and we keep acting surprised that it takes [441% longer](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways).当人类编写代码时，其背后的意图是显而易见的。各种方案的利弊经过权衡后被记录在开发者的脑海中，而审查过程则是对这些方案的再次确认。现代智能体在处理任务时也会进行推理，它们会留下推理的痕迹、权衡各种选项，并在过程中解释自己的决策过程。不过，问题在于，这些推理往往在代码被提交后就被丢弃了。它们很少被记录下来，也很难与代码一起被提交。而且，这些推理只是智能体关于如何完成任务的想法而已，而非人类对是否应该开始这项任务的判断。因此，审查过程从检查那些显而易见的推理，变成了试图理解那些从未被记录下来的意图。这显然更加困难且耗时，所以我们才会惊讶于为什么审查过程会花费多出 441%的时间。

A 2026 paper, [AI Slop and the Software Commons](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16754v1), analyzed 1,154 posts across 15 Reddit and Hacker News threads where developers discussed “AI slop”. One line from a developer caught my eye: reviewing an agent’s PR made them “the first human being to ever lay eyes on this code”.2026 年发表的一篇论文《AI 垃圾与软件共享资源》分析了 Reddit 和 Hacker News 上 15 个论坛中 1,154 篇关于“AI 垃圾”的帖子。其中，有一位开发者的评论引起了我的注意：他在审查某个智能体的代码时写道：“自己成了有史以来第一个看到这段代码的人。”

That points straight at the fix. **In normal review the author already understood the change and you were checking their work. With an agent PR, nobody has reconstructed the why yet. The reviewer is the first to try.** 这就直接指出了问题所在。在正常的审核过程中，审核者本来就已经理解了其中的变更，而审核人的任务就是确认这些变更是否正确。但在通过代理机构进行的公关处理中，目前还没有人试图去弄清楚其中的原因。审核者是第一个尝试去理解其中缘由的人。

As the paper puts it, review “wasn’t built to recover missing intent”. The encouraging part is that missing intent is recoverable: the reasoning existed, we just discarded it. Have the agent state what it was trying to do and what it ruled out, capture that [as a decision log](https://addyosmani.com/blog/intent-debt/) on the PR, and a large part of the reconstruction cost disappears. This is a tooling problem, and tooling problems get solved.正如该论文所指出的，这种审查机制“并非旨在弥补那些被遗漏的意图”。不过，令人欣慰的是，那些被遗漏的意图其实是可以被弥补的：相关的推理过程本来是存在的，只不过被我们忽略了而已。只要让智能体说明自己原本想做什么、又排除了哪些可能性，把这些信息记录在 PR 中，那么大部分的重建工作就迎刃而解了。这其实是个工具层面的问题，而工具层面的问题总归是可以解决的。

None of which makes “have the AI review the AI” a complete answer on its own. A second model with different priors genuinely catches real bugs, and it catches a lot of them, which is why you should run one. What it does not supply is the human judgment about whether this is the right change to build in the first place. That judgment stays with a person, and it happens to be the most interesting part of the job, the part worth keeping.这些方法都无法让“让人工智能来审查另一个人工智能”的做法成为一种完整的解决方案。另一个具有不同先验条件的模型确实能够发现真正的错误，而且能发现很多错误。因此，我们确实应该使用这样的模型。不过，这种模型无法替代人类的判断——即判断某项更改是否真的有必要被采纳。这种判断需要由人类来做出，而这正是这项工作中最有趣的部分，也是值得保留的部分。

## The tools are good, but not always for the reason they advertise这些工具确实不错，但并不总是像广告里宣传的那样好用。

**The current AI reviewers are genuinely good, and they occasionally don’t flag the same lines as each other, so the right move is not picking the best one but running two that are built differently.目前的 AI 审核员表现得相当不错。它们有时不会对同一行内容进行标记。因此，正确的做法不是只选择其中最优秀的那个审核结果，而是同时使用两个采用不同处理方式的审核结果。**

The dedicated AI review tools are good now, and I think you should be running at least your main coding agent if not a dedicated review agent on everything, side projects included. 现在，专门用于代码审查的 AI 工具已经相当不错了。我觉得，无论是在主要的项目中，还是在各种副项目中，你都应该至少使用专门的代码审查工具来辅助编程工作。

[CodeRabbit](https://www.coderabbit.ai/) is the most widely deployed and topped the independent [Martian benchmark](https://www.coderabbit.ai/blog/coderabbit-tops-martian-code-review-benchmark) (January to February 2026) on F1, around 49% precision with the best recall in the field. [Greptile](https://www.greptile.com/) trades precision for recall: around an 82% bug-catch rate against CodeRabbit’s 44% in one benchmark, at the cost of more false positives. [Anthropic’s Code Review](https://claude.com/blog/code-review) reports under 1% of its findings marked incorrect by their engineers, and the figure I would actually show a manager: it raised their internal rate of PRs receiving a substantive review from 16% to 54%. The long tail of changes that used to get a glance and an approval now gets read by something.CodeRabbit 是应用最广泛的工具。在 2026 年 1 月至 2 月的独立测试中，CodeRabbit 在 F1 测试中的准确率约为 49%，同时拥有该领域中最高的召回率。Greptile 则牺牲了准确率来提升召回率：在某项测试中，Greptile 的错误检测率为 82%，而 CodeRabbit 仅为 44%；不过，Greptile 的误报率也更高。Anthropic 的 Code Review 工具显示，其被工程师标记为错误的发现中，实际上只有不到 1%是被确认有误的。不过，我向经理们展示的数据是：该工具使得需要经过详细审查的代码比例从 16%上升到了 54%。过去那些只是被简单查看并获准通过的代码，现在都经过了更仔细的审查。

The most useful result I have seen this year is not from a vendor. An engineer [ran four reviewers in parallel](https://dev.to/_vjk/best-ai-code-reviewer-in-2026-we-ran-4-in-parallel-for-3-weeks-146-prs-679-findings-1c0f), CodeRabbit, Sentry Seer, Greptile and Cursor BugBot, across 146 real PRs and 679 findings over three and a half weeks:今年我发现的最有用的成果并非来自任何一家软件供应商。一位工程师让四款代码审查工具——CodeRabbit、Sentry Seer、Greptile 和 Cursor BugBot——同时对 146 个真实的代码提交进行了审查。在三个半的时间里，这些工具共发现了 679 个问题。

> Of 617 distinct flagged locations, **93.4% were caught by exactly one of the four tools**. 6% by two. Almost none by three. **None at all by all four.**在 617 个被标记的地点中，93.4%的地点只被其中一种工具检测到。6%的地点被两种工具检测到。几乎没有地点被三种工具检测到。完全没有地点被全部四种工具检测到。

The four tools never once flagged the same line. Each was strong at a different class of problem: Greptile with near-zero false positives on correctness and architecture, CodeRabbit with the widest net and one-click fixes, Seer best on production-failure severity. That is the adversarial review argument demonstrated on a real codebase rather than in a paper. Heterogeneity is the whole point. Four copies of one model is a single reviewer with a larger invoice, whereas four genuinely different reviewers surface a set of bugs no single member could find alone, the human included.这四种工具从未对同一行代码给出过相同的判断结果。每种工具在处理不同类型的问题时各有所长：Greptile 在判断代码的正确性和架构方面几乎不会产生误报；CodeRabbit 则具备更广泛的检测范围，并能实现一键修复；Seer 则最擅长识别生产环境中的严重故障。这就是在真实代码库上验证的“对抗性审查”机制，而非仅仅停留在理论层面。多样性正是这种机制的精髓所在。使用同一个模型的四个副本，相当于让一个“审查员”承担更多工作；而让四个真正不同的审查员来处理代码，则能发现那些单个审查员无法发现的错误，包括人类审查员在内。

In practice: do not agonize over the single best tool, there isn’t one. At the high-stakes end, run two with deliberately different characters (the experiment above paired Greptile for everyday correctness with Seer for production-failure severity, with almost no overlap). If you are solo, one good reviewer plus real tests is plenty. And whatever the marketing says, measure it on your own code, because every one of these results was specific to a particular codebase, and yours will be too.在实践中：不必纠结于哪种工具最理想，因为根本不存在所谓的“最佳工具”。在关键场景下，可以同时使用两种特性截然不同的工具来进行测试（比如，上述实验中，Greptile 负责确保代码的日常正确性，而 Seer 则用于检测可能导致的严重错误，两者几乎不会产生重叠）。如果你独自进行测试，那么一名优秀的测试人员加上实际的测试操作就足够了。无论营销宣传如何，都应以你自己的代码作为测试依据——因为所有这些测试结果都是针对特定代码库得出的，你的代码也是如此。

## Should we just let AI review more of it?我们是否应该让人工智能来处理更多这类任务呢？

**The machine is already reviewing more of your code than you are. The only real decision left is whether you do that deliberately, and the amount of human you keep should scale with your blast radius.这台机器审查的代码量已经远远超过了你所能处理的量。现在唯一需要决定的就是：你是否主动让机器来处理这些代码？此外，你需要保留多少人工干预的环节，也应该根据你的工作范围来调整。**

I keep hearing a question that would have been heresy a year ago, now from experienced engineers: should the machine be doing more of the reviewing, perhaps most of it? I no longer think that is a foolish question.我不断听到这样的问题：在一年前，提出这样的问题简直是不可想象的，但现在，连经验丰富的工程师也在提出这样的疑问——是否应该让机器来承担更多的审核工作，甚至全部审核工作？我不再认为这是一个愚蠢的问题了。

The uncomfortable part is that AI review works. Under 1% of Anthropic’s findings are marked wrong, the tools catch bugs humans read straight past, and they do not get tired on the thirtieth PR of the day, which is exactly when a human is least reliable. Meanwhile humans are visibly not keeping up: zero-review merges are up 31% and review times are up triple digits. In a real sense the machine is already reviewing more of the code than we are. The honest framing is not “should we let AI review more” but “AI is already doing it, are we going to be deliberate about that or let it happen by default while pretending humans still read everything”.令人不安的是，人工智能的代码审查确实很有效。在 Anthropic 的检测结果中，只有不到 1%被标记为错误。这些工具能够发现人类容易忽略的错误，而且它们不会因为一天要审查 30 次代码而感到疲劳——而恰恰在这一点上，人类的效率最低。与此同时，人类的工作进度明显跟不上：无需人工审查的代码合并比例上升了 31%，而审查所需的时间则增加了三倍多。从实际来看，现在处理代码的量，人工智能已经超过了人类。与其问“是否应该让人工智能承担更多审查工作”，不如说“人工智能已经在这么做了，我们是应该主动去利用这一优势，还是继续假装一切仍由人类来处理呢？”

[Loop engineering](https://addyosmani.com/blog/loop-engineering/) sharpens this. The premise of a loop is that you stop being the person who prompts the agent and instead build a system that prompts it, and a central part of that system is a judge: an agent that decides whether the work is done before moving on. The reviewer is the next role being designed out of the inner loop, on purpose. We spent a year automating the writing, and the loops are now automating the checking, and the human keeps getting pushed up and out. “Where does the human stay” is not a seminar question, it is something you decide every time you wire up a loop, whether or not you realize you are deciding it.循环机制进一步强化了这一过程。循环机制的核心理念是：不再由人来主动提示智能体该做什么，而是构建一个能够自动提示智能体的系统。而该系统的核心部分就是“审核者”——也就是那个负责判断任务是否已完成后再让系统继续执行的智能体。所谓“审核者”，其实也是循环机制中的另一个重要组成部分。我们花了整整一年时间来让写作过程自动化，现在则让审核过程也实现自动化。这样一来，人类的作用就越来越被边缘化了。其实，“人类应该扮演什么角色？”并不是什么复杂的问题，每次你设计循环机制时，都需要自己来决定这个问题，哪怕你并没有意识到自己正在做这个决定。

Where I currently land is: the answer may not be “a human reads every line”. That is over. But it is also not “let the loop review itself and walk away”. When an agent writes the code, another reviews it and a third judges it, you have a closed loop of models with broadly correlated blind spots, especially when they come from the same family, confidently agreeing in the same places. A confident “looks good” with no human anywhere in it is [borrowed confidence](https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-surrender/): the system’s certainty becomes yours, and nobody actually understood anything. The loop can be both very sure and very wrong, with no human left to tell the difference.我目前的结论是：答案很可能不是“由人类来逐行检查代码”。那种做法已经过时了。但同时，也不能让代码审查过程仅仅依靠循环自我检测就了事。当某个程序员编写代码后，另一个人负责审查，第三个人则对代码进行最终评估。这样一来，就形成了一个由多个模型构成的循环系统。不过，这些模型的“盲点”往往是相互关联的——尤其是当它们来自同一个“家族”时，它们往往会一致地认为代码没有问题。这种基于系统的“肯定判断”其实并不可靠：系统的确定性其实并不等于人类的理解力。最终，这个循环系统可能得出完全正确的结论，也可能犯下严重错误，而没有人能够分辨其中的差别。

So the human does not leave; the human moves up a level. You stop reviewing every diff and start owning the parts that do not transfer to a model. Accountability matters.所以，人并没有离开；人只是上升到了更高的层次而已。你不再需要逐一检查所有的差异，而是开始专注于那些无法被纳入模型的部分。责任心非常重要。

The judgment of whether this is even the right change to build, as distinct from whether the code is correct. The high-blast-radius gates where being wrong is expensive. And the awkward one: the behavior nobody specified, because a model reviews the code that exists and rarely flags the requirement that nobody thought to write down, which remains [a human-shaped gap](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/) I do not expect to close soon. 这其实是一个关于“是否应该进行这样的更改”的判断，而与代码本身是否正确是两回事。那些影响范围很大的功能，一旦出错，后果会很严重。还有那些没人明确规定的功能：因为模型只是审查现有的代码，而很少会指出那些没人想到要写进代码里的要求。这显然是一个人为造成的漏洞，我觉得短期内很难解决。

Human in the loop becomes human on the loop: sampling, spot-checking and auditing the system rather than reading every PR, and spending your limited attention where being wrong would actually hurt.“人在循环中”的理念意味着：对系统进行抽样检查、随机审核，而不是阅读每一条提交给系统的更改请求。这样，就能把有限的精力集中在那些出错可能会造成严重后果的地方。

This is already how I work on my own projects, including the open-source ones that now see more PRs in a day than I could carefully read in an evening. 这就是我处理自己所负责的项目的方式，包括那些开源项目。现在，这些开源项目每天收到的提交请求数量之多，让我根本无法在一天之内全部仔细查看。

**I point Claude Code or Codex at a batch of incoming PRs and ask for a first pass: a high-level read of what looks safe to merge, what needs more work, and what is genuinely high-risk. I do not auto-merge on the result, and I do not lazy-merge whatever it approves. What it gives me is a way to allocate attention. 我会用 Claude Code 或 Codex 来分析一批待处理的合并请求。我会先进行初步评估：判断哪些内容可以安全地合并，哪些还需要进一步处理，以及哪些属于高风险的内容。我不会自动合并这些请求，也不会盲目地接受所有被评估为“可合并”的请求。这个工具帮助我合理分配精力。**

I can spend a few minutes confirming the changes it considers low-risk, and put real, careful time into the ones it flags as dangerous. The detail that matters is that this is not my old review hour made slightly faster. It is a different shape of hour, and at the volume I now deal with, it is the main reason the queue stays survivable at all.对于那些被系统判定为低风险的变更，我可以花几分钟时间来确认；而对于那些被标记为有风险的变更，则会投入更多的时间和精力来处理。重要的是，这已经不再是我以前那种以更快速度完成审查的方式了。现在的工作方式完全不同了。以我目前的工作量来看，这种方式正是确保工作流程能够正常运转的关键所在。

📷 Codex and Claude Code giving me a first-pass, risk-sorted read of a batch of PRs. The triage is the help. The merge decision stays mine.📷 Codex 和 Claude Code 帮助我初步筛选了一堆待处理的 PR，识别出其中的风险点。这些工具确实很有用。不过，最终是否合并这些 PR 还是由我来决定。

A more extreme version of the same move is Kun Chen, an [ex-Meta L8 engineer now shipping around 40 PRs a day as a solo builder, who has largely stopped reviewing code](https://creatoreconomy.so/p/how-this-ex-meta-l8-engineer-ships-40-prs-a-day-with-ai-kun-chen) as told to [@petergyang](https://x.com/@petergyang). It would be easy to dismiss this, except he is an L8, unusually good at the thing he stopped doing, which is what makes it interesting. He runs 20 to 30 agents in parallel and has moved his effort into the plan: he writes detailed plans up front, the agents run for hours against them, and he says plan quality determines how long they can run unattended. That is the move I described above. It is worth being precise about what actually happened, because it is not that he stopped verifying. The intent did not vanish, he wrote it down himself in the plan, so the “first human to ever lay eyes on this” problem is half-solved: a human did understand the why, just up front rather than after. And he did not work without a net, he built an automated review gate (he calls it No Mistakes) that checks the code before it merges, and he stays on escalation when an agent gets stuck. The human does the expensive thinking before the code exists and the machine does the line-by-line afterward, which may well be the shape of where this goes.同一策略的极端版本就是 Kun Chen。他曾经是 Meta 的 L8 工程师，现在以独立开发者的身份每天发布大约 40 个代码修改。按照@petergyang 的建议，他几乎不再审查代码了。这种做法很容易被忽视，但问题在于，Kun Chen 原本就是 L8 级别的专家，他在那些他不再从事的工作上依然表现出色，这正是他的厉害之处。他同时运行 20 到 30 个自动化脚本来执行任务。他会事先制定详细的计划，让这些脚本按照计划运行数小时。他认为，计划的质量决定了这些脚本能够在无人监控的情况下持续运行多久。这就是我上面所描述的策略。有必要准确描述实际情况：他并没有停止对代码的验证工作。他的意图依然存在，只不过他把这些意图写进了计划中而已。这样一来，“第一个了解这个计划的人是谁”的问题就得到了一半的解决：至少有人事先理解了计划的意图。而且，他做事时也并非毫无准备。他开发了一个自动代码审查系统（他称之为“无错误”），该系统会在代码被合并之前对其进行检查。当人工处理遇到困难时，他会亲自介入处理。在代码生成之前，由人类来进行复杂的思考；而代码生成之后，则由机器来逐行检查代码。这很可能是未来代码审查的发展方向。

But he is a solo builder with no large team and no decade-old system full of landmines beneath him. The exact conditions that make 40 PRs a day without review rational for him are conditions most readers do not have. Copy his workflow onto a team shipping to many users and you reproduce the Faros numbers on your own dashboard. He is not wrong; he is a long way down one specific end of the spectrum.但他是个独自工作的人，没有庞大的团队，也没有那些充满各种隐患的、运作了数十年的旧有体系。让他能够每天处理 40 条 PR 而无需经过审核的条件，是大多数读者所不具备的。如果把他的工作流程应用到需要为大量用户提供服务的团队中，那么你的仪表板上也会显示出与 Faros 相同的数字。他并没有错；只不过他处于这个光谱上的某个极端而已。

Which is the spectrum point again. Solo with no users: letting AI review almost all of it is a defensible 2026 position, and you should not feel guilty about it. Maintaining something large for many people: let the machine handle the first pass, the second pass and the boring 90%, but keep a real human on the load-bearing paths and do not let the loop close completely on anything that can hurt someone. How much human you keep is a dial, and you set it by blast radius, not by guilt.那么，哪个才是正确的处理方式呢？对于没有用户的情况，让人工智能来处理所有事务，这在 2026 年是完全可行的做法，你不必为此感到内疚。而对于需要多人共同处理的任务，可以让机器来处理初步的处理工作、那些繁琐的环节，但那些关键环节则必须由真人来处理。对于那些可能对他人造成伤害的环节，绝不能完全依赖人工智能来处理。需要多少人工干预，可以根据具体情况来决定，而不是凭主观上的内疚感来决定。

## What to actually do到底该怎么做呢

**Stop reviewing everything to the same depth. Spend scarce human attention only where being wrong is costly, and let cheap deterministic gates and AI reviewers handle the rest.不必对所有事情都进行同样深入的审查。把宝贵的人力精力集中在那些出错会带来严重后果的领域上吧，至于其他事情，就交给那些成本低廉的自动审查系统来处理吧。**

The organizing idea is to match review effort to the cost of being wrong, push the cheap deterministic work as early as possible, and reserve human attention for what only humans can do.其核心理念是：让审核工作的投入与犯错的代价相称，尽可能把那些简单、重复性的工作交给机器来完成，从而让人类能够把精力集中在那些只有人类才能完成的任务上。

**Tier by risk, not by author.** A config change earns a linter and a glance. A revision to your core business logic path earns the full stack: types, tests, two different AI reviewers, a human who owns that system, and a security pass. Do not spend a heavy review on boilerplate, and do not wave through a big change because the tests are green. The [layered approach](https://addyosmani.com/blog/verification-bottleneck/) is the same everywhere; what changes is how many layers a given diff has to clear.应按风险级别来处理各项变更，而非根据变更的发起者来决定处理方式。对于单纯的配置更改，只需进行简单的检查即可。但如果涉及到核心业务逻辑的修改，那么就需要进行全面的审查：包括类型检查、测试、由不同的人工智能系统进行的审核、由该系统的负责人亲自检查，以及安全方面的验证。不要在那些无关紧要的代码上花费太多精力进行审查；同时，也不要因为测试结果正常就轻易通过那些重大的变更。这种分层处理的方式在任何地方都是一样的；不同的是，每项变更需要通过多少层审查而已。

**Fast-fail the expensive tail.** The most useful recent finding for teams drowning in agent PRs is [Early-Stage Prediction of Review Effort](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00753) (January 2026), which studied 33,707 agent-authored PRs. Agents are good at small, well-defined changes, around 28% merge almost instantly, but they tend to “ghost” the moment they get subjective feedback, abandoning the back-and-forth that review actually is. (A companion 2026 paper found [reviewer abandonment accounted for 38% of rejected agent PRs](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15195).) The researchers built a “circuit breaker” that predicts high-maintenance PRs from cheap signals like file types and patch size before a human looks, and it works well. Triage agent PRs up front, fast-track the trivial ones, and do not let a person sink an hour into a sprawling change the agent will abandon as soon as you push back.快速处理那些成本高昂的修改请求吧。对于那些被大量代码修改请求搞得不堪重负的团队来说，2026 年 1 月发表的一项研究结果非常有价值。该研究分析了 33,707 个由自动化工具提交的修改请求。自动化工具擅长处理那些规模较小、定义明确的修改；大约 28%的修改请求可以立即被合并。不过，一旦收到主观性的反馈，自动化工具就会放弃进一步的修改，不再参与后续的讨论过程。（另一篇 2026 年的研究指出，38%被拒绝的修改请求都是因为自动化工具放弃了处理。）研究人员开发了一种“断路器”机制，能够根据文件类型、修改范围等简单指标来预判哪些修改请求需要人工处理。这一机制效果不错。可以先对自动化工具提交的修改请求进行分类处理：那些简单的修改请求可以直接处理；而那些复杂的修改请求则不必浪费时间去处理，因为自动化工具很可能会在稍后放弃这些修改。

**Raise the bar for what you will even review.** The fix for being buried is not locking down the repository, it is [refusing to review changes that arrive without evidence](https://www.builder.io/blog/developers-drowning-in-ai-prs). Require, before review: a statement of what the change is for, a diff that is not 3,500 lines with no comments, the test output, and proof it was actually run. This is how you stop being the first human to read the code. You push the intent-reconstruction work back onto whoever submitted it, where it is cheap, rather than absorbing it yourself, where it is expensive.提高你对所审查内容的标准吧。要解决“被淹没在大量代码中”的问题，不是把代码库锁起来，而是拒绝审查那些没有附带任何说明的代码更改。在审查之前，必须要求提交者提供以下信息：更改的目的、详细的代码差异对比（而不是长达 3,500 行且没有任何注释的代码）、测试结果，以及证明代码确实已经被执行过的证据。只有这样，你才能避免成为第一个阅读这些代码的人。把代码解读的工作交给提交者来做吧，这样成本更低；而不是自己来处理，因为那样成本太高。

**Keep PRs small, deliberately.** Agent PRs run large, [51% larger on average](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways) in the Faros data, and reviewer engagement is one of the strongest predictors that a PR merges at all. A large unreviewable PR gets [rejected outright](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/) or, worse, rubber-stamped. Instruct your agents to produce small commits. A diff a human can actually read is now a design constraint, not a courtesy.请刻意将提交的大小控制在较小范围内。在 Faros 的数据中，由代理程序提交的提交规模要大得多——平均而言要大 51%。而审核者的参与程度则是决定提交是否会被合并的最重要因素之一。那些过大且无法被审核的提交要么会被直接拒绝，要么就会被草率地批准。请指示您的代理程序提交较小的代码量。现在，提交的内容必须便于人类阅读，这已经是一种设计要求，而非一种额外的礼节。

**Read the test changes more carefully than the code.** This is the agent failure mode to watch. The agent changes behavior, then “fixes” the test by rewriting the assertion to match the new, broken behavior. A green check over 200 edited tests means nothing until you have confirmed the edits were correct. Treat any diff that rewrites many tests as a flag and read those first. Mutation testing earns its place here: coverage tells you a line ran, mutation testing tells you whether the test would notice if that line were wrong.请比阅读代码时更仔细地检查那些测试上的更改。这是需要重点关注的代理程序故障模式。代理程序会改变其行为，然后通过重新编写测试用例来“修复”这些错误，好让测试结果看起来正常。不过，即便有 200 多个测试用例通过了检查，也并不意味着一切正常，除非你确认了这些更改确实是正确的。如果某个更改涉及大量测试用例的修改，那一定要格外注意，先仔细检查这些更改。变异测试在这里非常重要：代码覆盖率只能告诉你某行代码是否被执行过了，而变异测试则能告诉你：如果某行代码出错了，测试是否能够检测出来。

**Treat CI as the wall that does not move.** Watch for the patterns [GitHub now warns reviewers about](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/agent-pull-requests-are-everywhere-heres-how-to-review-them/): removed tests, skipped lint, lowered coverage thresholds, a duplicated helper that already exists elsewhere, and untrusted input flowing into a prompt. That last one deserves emphasis, because agent-built features are a fresh source of [prompt injection](https://simonwillison.net/series/prompt-injection/): if a change pipes user-controlled text into an LLM call without thinking about what that text can instruct the model to do, the vulnerability is not visible in the diff, it is latent in the data that will arrive later. Agents will also weaken CI to make themselves pass, not maliciously, just gradient descent finding the cheapest path to green. Deterministic gates are the one part of the pipeline that cannot be talked out of their verdict by a confident paragraph, so keep them strict.把持续集成系统视为不可更改的“墙”。请注意 GitHub 现在会向审查人员警示的那些问题：被移除的测试用例、被跳过的代码检查、被降低的代码覆盖率阈值、重复存在的辅助函数，以及被直接输入到模型中的不可信数据。最后一点尤其重要，因为由智能体生成的代码往往是注入恶意指令的途径。如果某个更改将用户输入的文本直接传递给大语言模型，而不对该文本可能导致的后果进行考虑，那么这种漏洞在代码差异对比中是无法被发现的，但它会隐藏在后续处理的数据中。智能体还会试图削弱持续集成系统的检测机制，以便让自身的代码通过检测——当然，这并非出于恶意，只是因为智能体在优化过程中会寻找最简单的“路径”来达成目标。在持续集成流程中，只有确定性逻辑门是无法被轻易绕过的，因此必须严格遵守其规则。

**A human owns the merge.** A model cannot be paged and cannot be held responsible for what it shipped, so whoever clicks merge owns it. When an AI review says “looks good” in a calm, confident voice, it is handing you [confidence it has not necessarily earned](https://addyosmani.com/blog/cognitive-surrender/). Treat every AI review as a sensor, not a verdict: data, not a decision.合并操作由人类来掌控。模型无法被“调用”，也无法对自身产生的结果负责。因此，无论谁点击了“合并”按钮，该结果就归该人所有。当人工智能以平静而自信的语气评价“看起来不错”时，它其实只是把本来不一定属于自己的自信传递给了你。请把人工智能的每一条评价都视为一种数据反馈，而非最终结论：它们只是数据，而非决策。

If you are solo with no users, the tiering, the test-change discipline and CI are most of what you need; the rest is overhead until people show up. If you are the large organization, all of it is the baseline, and the triage and intake bar are the difference between a review process that scales and one that quietly collapses.如果你独自一人开发，没有其他用户，那么分层管理、测试与变更管理以及持续集成这些功能就足够了；其余的则属于不必要的开销，直到有其他人加入进来。如果你身处大型组织中，那么所有这些功能都是基本要求。而如何有效地处理各种任务和需求，则决定了整个系统的可扩展性，也决定了系统能否正常运转。

## What this means if you run a team如果你负责管理一个团队，那这意味着什么？

**The bottleneck is no longer how fast you write code, it is how fast a trusted human can be confident in a review. Cutting the people who provide that confidence because “AI made us faster” simply converts the saving into future incidents.现在的瓶颈不再在于编写代码的速度，而在于人类在代码审查过程中能够多快地做出可靠的判断。因为“人工智能让我们变得更高效”而削减那些能够提供可靠判断的人手，只会把原本节省下来的时间浪费在将来可能出现的各种问题上。**

The binding constraint on shipping is no longer how fast you can write code. It is how fast a trusted human can be confident a change is correct. Any plan that treats generation as the bottleneck and review as free will quietly stall, with the velocity dashboard staying green the whole way.在软件开发过程中，制约效率的因素不再是编写代码的速度，而是可靠的人士能够多快地确认某项更改是正确的。任何将代码生成视为瓶颈、而将代码审查视为可有可无的流程，最终都会导致项目进展缓慢。即便如此，速度监控仪表盘上的数值依然会保持“正常”状态。

The Faros report is direct about this: QA and review work rises even as output rises, so reducing engineering headcount because “AI made us faster” is dangerous unless you have closed the review gap first. The senior-engineer tax, review time up by triple digits, falls hardest on the people you can least afford to bottleneck, and it is invisible to any metric that only counts merged PRs.Faros 的报告明确指出了这一点：尽管产出增加了，但质量检测和审查的工作量却也在上升。因此，如果因为“人工智能让我们效率更高了”而削减工程人员数量，那将是极其危险的，除非先解决审查环节中的问题。所谓“高级工程师负担过重”的问题，意味着审查时间增加了数倍，而这种负担最沉重地落在了那些最不能承受效率低下的领域。不过，那些只统计合并提交的代码量的指标，根本无法反映这一情况。

Open source maintainers hit this wall first and hardest. The [steady stream of plausible but hollow contributions](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16754v1) costs real triage time even when it is well-intentioned, and that is the canary. Companies are next. The ones handling it well treat review capacity as a real resource to be measured, protected and spent deliberately, not as slack that AI has freed up.开源项目的维护者最先且最深刻地面临着这一难题。那些看似有用但实际上毫无价值的贡献不断涌现，即便这些贡献出于好意，也会占用宝贵的审查时间。这其实是个危险的信号。接下来受影响的就是各家公司。那些处理得当的公司会将审查能力视为一项需要被妥善管理、保护和合理利用的宝贵资源，而不会将其视为人工智能所释放出来的“闲置资源”。

## Writing got cheap, understanding didn’t写作变得容易了，但理解却没那么简单了。

**Code review did not become less important when agents arrived. It became the central activity. Writing code is increasingly solved and getting cheaper by the month; the durable advantage is the system that lets you trust what was written.随着智能体的出现，代码审查的重要性非但没有降低，反而成为了核心任务。编写代码的成本正在逐月降低；而最持久的优势在于：该系统能够确保人们能够信任所编写的代码。**

Do not take the one-size answer in either direction. If you are solo with no users, the enterprise horror stories about churn and duplication are a future risk, not today’s fire, so lean on your tests, review what matters, and stay honest that the deferred work is still owed. If you maintain something large for many people, every alarming number here is about you, and the only thing that holds is a tiered, evidence-required, deliberately heterogeneous review process with a human owning the merge.不要采取一刀切的解决方案。如果你独自一人负责整个系统，没有其他用户，那么那些关于用户流失和数据重复的问题，虽然可能是未来的风险，但眼下并不紧迫。因此，你应该依靠各项测试结果来做出决策，仔细评估各项指标的真实性。同时，也要坦诚地认识到，那些被推迟的工作仍然需要去完成。如果你负责管理的是供许多人使用的大型系统，那么每一个令人担忧的数字都与你息息相关。唯一有效的解决办法，就是建立一套多层次的、需要证据支持的审查流程，由专人负责最终的决定。

What is constant across the whole spectrum is the underlying economics. We made writing cheap, and understanding stayed exactly as expensive as it has always been. The teams that do well over the next few years will not be the ones generating the most code, they will be the ones who built a review system they can actually trust, and who never confuse “the tests passed” with “a person understands what this does and why”.

Or, as [@simonw](https://x.com/@simonw) keeps putting it, [your job is to deliver code you have proven to work](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/18/code-proven-to-work/). Agents have not changed that. They have made the proving the center of the job rather than an afterthought, and I think that is a good trade.

**Understanding a system well enough to stand behind it is the most durable and most interesting skill in software, and there has never been a better time to get extraordinarily good at it.**
